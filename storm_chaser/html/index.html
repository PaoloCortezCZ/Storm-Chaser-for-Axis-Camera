Camera connection

How Storm Chaser reaches your camera. Local: enter the camera IP, user and password (modern Axis cameras use digest auth, handled automatically). Cloud: tick "Use CamStreamer Cloud" and paste the device-connect.net URL + access token instead — useful when the camera isn't on your LAN.

Protocol & port: choose HTTP or HTTPS. Leave the port blank/0 to use the protocol default (80 for HTTP, 443 for HTTPS), or set a custom port if your camera listens elsewhere. HTTPS accepts self-signed / untrusted certificates, which is the usual case for Axis cameras on a LAN.

PTZ camera channel selects which video channel / view area the PTZ preset commands target (1 for most cameras).

Choosing a view area fills the PTZ channel, preset count and first preset # below.

Camera location

The geographic spot where the camera is mounted. This is the origin point from which the app measures the compass bearing (direction) and distance to each storm cell. If it's wrong, every direction will be off, so set it as precisely as you can — use the Quick-select for a known site or type exact latitude/longitude.

Bearings are degrees clockwise from north: 0°=N, 90°=E, 180°=S, 270°=W.

The exact spot the camera is mounted. Storm bearings are computed from here.

Preset coverage

How the camera is aimed at the storm.

  • PTZ presets on one view area — for a single PTZ camera. You spread N presets around the compass; the app sends the camera to the preset nearest the storm's bearing.
  • CamSwitcher views — switch between different cameras/view areas you set up in CamSwitcher. Give each view the direction it faces; the app activates the view pointing nearest the storm.

Coverage 360° = full circle. 180° = a one-sided arc (use when the camera/site only sees one half, e.g. coast or wall behind). Arc-center bearing is the direction the middle of that arc points.

Home preset/view is where it parks when no storm exceeds the trigger threshold (0 = stay put).

The camera moves to pan = storm bearing − reference, holds your fixed tilt, and zooms between "near" and "far" based on distance (near storms = wide, far = tele). Uses the PTZ channel set under Camera connection. Tick "Flip pan" if it turns the wrong way.
Pan reach limits how far the camera swings from its pan-0 bearing: e.g. facing North with 90° left + 120° right covers W→ESE and ignores storms behind those edges. Set both to 180 for a full 360° camera. "Left" is counter-clockwise (West when facing North), "right" is clockwise (East). These geographic limits don't change with "Flip pan".

For each view: the compass bearing it faces, and the full CamSwitcher playlist_switch.cgi URL (or just the playlist_name). The storm's direction picks the view with the nearest bearing. Each view has a color shown on the map.

Choose a view area & fetch (above or here), then assign a camera preset and the bearing it faces to each slot. The storm's direction picks the nearest preset. Each has a color on the map.

Storm detection

Every scan the app samples weather at a fan of points around the camera (free Open-Meteo data) and scores each for storm strength. The highest-scoring point is the "storm" it chases.

Scan radius — how far out (km) to look. Rings × Samples/ring — the probe grid density (e.g. 3×16 = 48 points); more = finer detail but a bit slower.

Intensity score = a weighted blend of four weather variables. Raise a weight to make that factor matter more:

  • Lightning potential (J/kg) — model estimate of how likely/strong lightning is. The most direct "thunderstorm" signal, but only some regional models report it (may be 0 elsewhere).
  • CAPE — Convective Available Potential Energy (J/kg): how much "fuel" the atmosphere has for thunderstorms. Rough guide: <1000 weak, 1000–2500 moderate, >2500 strong instability. Weighted low (×0.002) because the numbers are large.
  • Precipitation (mm) — current rainfall rate; matches what you see on the radar.
  • Wind gusts (km/h) — strong gusts often mark an active storm's leading edge.

Intensity trigger threshold — the minimum score before the camera bothers to chase; below it, the camera goes to Home/holds. Raise it to ignore light weather, lower it to react to weaker cells.

Tip: to follow the rain you see on radar, increase the precipitation weight and lower lightning/CAPE.

Below this score the camera goes to the home preset (or holds). Probe points: — · est. — API calls/day

Severe weather alerts — US (NWS)

Adds real National Weather Service warnings as a second source (free, no key, United States only). When enabled, active Tornado / Severe Thunderstorm / Hurricane / Tropical Storm warnings near the camera become high-priority targets — shown as purple dots on the map.

With override on, an active WARNING outranks the weather-model storm score, so the camera turns toward the warned area. Watches and weaker alerts rank below warnings. Outside the US, NWS returns nothing — leave this off.

Source: api.weather.gov · covers tornadoes, severe thunderstorms, hurricanes & tropical storms in the United States. For typhoons elsewhere (Japan, Asia-Pacific…), use the tropical-cyclone source below.

Tropical cyclones — worldwide (typhoons)

Tracks active typhoons and hurricanes anywhere on Earth using GDACS (UN/EU, free, no key) — this is the source to use for Japan and the Western Pacific and every other cyclone basin. When a cyclone's centre is within range, the app aims the camera at its bearing and shows it as a high-priority target. Alert level (Green/Orange/Red) and max wind decide which cyclone wins if several are active.

With override on, an in-range cyclone outranks the local weather-model storm score. Range is how far away a cyclone centre can be and still be tracked — typhoons are huge, so 1000–2000 km is reasonable (the camera only needs the bearing; zoom is capped for distant cells).

Tornadoes outside the US: there is no global keyless tornado-warning feed. The camera still finds tornadic supercells through the normal lightning/CAPE/precipitation scoring — just without a dedicated warning to override it.

Source: gdacs.org · global tropical-cyclone positions, updated every few hours. Covers Japan (typhoons), the Atlantic/Pacific hurricanes, Indian Ocean & South Pacific cyclones.

Timing & smoothing

Controls how often it looks and how eagerly it moves.

  • Scan interval — minutes between checks. Set 1–2 for responsive chasing (the underlying weather data itself refreshes about every 10–15 min).
  • Min dwell — minimum time to stay on a view before a routine switch, so the camera doesn't flip back and forth constantly.
  • Hysteresis — how far (degrees) a storm must move past a sector edge before switching. Prevents jitter when a storm sits right between two views.
  • Switch margin — an override: if a clearly stronger storm appears in another sector (this much stronger, e.g. 0.25 = 25%), the camera switches immediately without waiting out the dwell.

Re-checks every "scan interval" minutes (set 1 for fastest). Dwell + hysteresis stop jitter between adjacent presets; but a storm this much stronger (0.25 = 25%) in another sector overrides the dwell and switches immediately.

CamOverlay InfoTicker (optional)

Optionally burn a live status line onto the video via CamOverlay's InfoTicker. Enter the InfoTicker service ID from your CamOverlay config, then write a template using these variables: {status}, {bearing}, {compass}, {distance}, {intensity}, {preset}, {location}, {time}. Leave disabled if you don't use CamOverlay.

Vars: {status} {bearing} {compass} {distance} {dist_unit} {intensity} {preset} {location} {time}

CamOverlay Custom Graphics (optional)

Push each value to a field of a CamOverlay Custom Graphics service (so you can design a styled widget instead of a scrolling line). Enter the service ID, then the field name for each variable you want shown (e.g. field1, field2…). Leave a field blank to skip it. Field names must match those defined in your CamOverlay Custom Graphics layout.
